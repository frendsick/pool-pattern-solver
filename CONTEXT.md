# Pool Pattern Solver

A 9-ball pattern-play trainer: generates random late-rack layouts, solves for the best run-out pattern, and draws it in the style of the PoolDawg pattern-play diagrams (pie-shaped position zones, arrowed cue-ball paths).

## Language

**Pattern**:
A complete run-out plan: the ball-in-hand cue-ball placement, then for each remaining ball the chosen pocket, the cue-ball route to the next shot, and the targeted position zone.
_Avoid_: run, sequence, order (order is forced by 9-ball rules and is not a decision)

**Layout**:
A randomly generated set of remaining object-ball positions (no cue ball — the player has ball in hand) that the solver takes as input.
_Avoid_: position (reserved for cue-ball position play), rack

**Ball in Hand**:
The starting condition of every Layout: the solver chooses the opening cue-ball placement as the first decision of the Pattern.

**Position Zone**:
The pie-shaped region of cue-ball positions from which the next ball can be made to its chosen pocket AND the cue ball can still be moved on toward the following ball's zone (onward control); bounded by obstacle balls, pocketability/cut limits (max cut 60°, beyond a quarter-ball hit ~48° only within ~1 m), a ~13 cm rail band excluded only where the shot would cue AWAY from the near cushion toward the table center (the bridge hand lands on the rail; shooting along — or into — the near rail is comfortable however close the cushion sits, and such positions stay in the zone) unless the zone exists nowhere else, and a ~25 cm clearance from the object ball itself (a cue ball finishing on top of the next ball is cramped, hard inside ~10 cm) (PoolDawg's "pie-shaped diagram"). The drawn zone is where you'd be HAPPY, not everywhere the shot merely exists: only positions within 80% of the best the zone offers count. Onward control discounts an exit by the travel the pot FORCES on the cue ball (pocket pace at the cut angle, exp(-t/~115 cm)) and by the exit type's execution reliability (draw is always the toughest); travel chosen beyond the forced minimum costs nothing — except the hit power it demands (see Route), which prices long exits off near-straight shots out — so the window stays long along the line of the shot, away from the object ball, and natural multi-rail routes count fully. Zones via pockets other than the chosen one are drawn fainter as second-choice expansions, held to the primary pocket's quality bar. The route search compares pockets and shot types on one EFFECTIVE scale: each landing's zone value priced by the route's ease (the type's execution reliability, the hit power the chosen travel demands at that cut, draw rail-room), and a landing counts only within 80% of the best effective value the node can reach over every pocket × type × travel (per-line fallback bars when nothing clears it, so layouts still solve). The closest/easiest pocket still sets the standard, but the hardest shot's access to a zone's richest spot can no longer prune an easy natural follow into a merely decent stretch (2026-06-11 round 8: a long draw chosen over the player's natural-angle follow; 2026-06-12: a tiny window via the planned route when a huge one was reachable). The route search runs on the SAME onward-control-gated zones the renderer draws (made affordable by memoizing onward control per zone — it varies only with the cut angle, its side, and draw distance, since every exit traces from the fixed ghost ball), and its strict pass keeps landings out of the rail no-go band with a small inset. The drawn window's quality bar is capped to what the chosen route's landing stretch can actually reach (PlannedShot.windowRef): the window shows the stretch that route is playing for, and the planned landing always sits inside it (2026-06-11 screenshot report: a landing in the dead gap between two zone lobes). When the shot is easy and quite short, the headroom goes to AGGRESSION (2026-06-12 round 10): the landing candidates always include the deep end of the route's in-window stretch — usually its closest approach to the next ball — backed off the window's edge by 2σ of landing spread, never to the literal edge; the spread quadrature prices the margin, so the deep landing (maximum draw included, now realistically priced when short) wins exactly when getting 0.5–1 m closer turns a missable next shot into an effectively unmissable one.
_Avoid_: shape zone, window (see Flagged ambiguities)

**Route**:
How the cue ball travels from one shot to the next: a Shot Type plus rails contacted, with travel distance (speed) as the free parameter — bounded below by pocket pace (the object ball must still reach the pocket with margin, so the cue ball cannot travel less than its physics share of that speed) and priced above by hit power: the cue ball keeps only the sin²(cut) + k²·cos²(cut) share of the hit's distance budget (k = the type's roll/draw fraction, 2/7 for follow and draw), so the chosen travel implies an equivalent hit roll-out (hitDistance); the route's value decays past a comfortable stroke (~150″) and dies at the ceiling (~700″). A near-straight shot keeps almost nothing — sending the cue ball any real distance sideways off one would need a monster stroke that also wrecks the pot, so such routes are not shots.

**Shot Type**:
One of five idealized cue-ball actions — stop, follow (natural roll angle), stun (tangent line), touch of low (slight draw, just off the tangent), draw — that fixes the cue ball's departure direction off the object ball; sidespin is deferred to a later version (see ADR-0001). The PATH into that direction is the 30-degree-rule shape (ADR-0003): off the ball the cue ball rides the TANGENT LINE and cloth friction bends it on a small parabola into the carom line — the shape is speed-invariant and scales with the chosen travel, so a harder shot stays on the tangent line farther in absolute inches. Follow visibly bends forward, draw hooks back, stun stays on the tangent; drawn routes, scratch checks and obstacle clearance near the object ball use the real curved path (caromCurve), while candidate landings are walked along the straight landing locus the scaling implies (caromLocus). Each type has an execution reliability (stop 0.99 … draw 0.85 at ~1 m) that multiplies a route's P(position): draw is always the toughest of the available shots, gets worse beyond ~1 m of cue-to-ball distance (it needs a much harder stroke) but EASES below it (drawShortEase: keeping backspin over half a meter is routine, ~0.92 — only the clean-action reliability eases, the landing spread keeps its draw-sized sigmas, so draw stays the hardest type to land precisely), and needs ROOM for the backspin to act — a departure line meeting a cushion within ~10″ compromises the action (drawRailFactor; a touch of low half as much). The carom DIRECTION each type leaves is differently controllable (caromDirSigma, folded into the landing spread): with aim error e the impact line rotates by the amplified A·e, and the departure rotates by a′(cut)·e + (1−a′)·A·e where a′ = d/dc atan(tan c / k). For a rolling follow a′ = 1 exactly at the ~28° natural angle, so the carom direction error collapses to the bare aim error — the 30°-rule plateau, and why a 15–30° cut is the sweet spot for MOVING the cue ball sideways (2026-06-12 feedback) while the kill-angle sweet spot (stopping near in place) is under ~10°, where the stop shot lives. Stun carries the full amplified error, draw more, and a near-straight follow is twitchy (a′ = 3.5) — capped by what the pocket window admits, since bigger errors are misses, not position errors. Routes that skim a pocket mouth carry a scratch-risk penalty — but only where the path ARRIVES within the pocket's acceptance cone: the jaws funnel a ball heading into the mouth, while a pass beyond the acceptance angle (e.g. rebounding off the cushion half a diamond from a side pocket) meets cushion-backed facings and is safe (2026-06-12 round 12: the distance-only penalty shaved a bottom-rail follow that comes back up the next shot's line — the play a player would choose from ball in hand — and let a left-rail follow that merely crosses the window win). Extra travel is only a tie-break, NOT a cost: even a short positional touch carries a ~3–4″ landing floor (speedSigmaFloor — a small window is never guaranteed), so going longer on a natural angle for a significantly bigger window, or to come in along the next shot's line, is often the easier play, and the score's landing-distribution math decides it. Pros drive the cue ball into a cushion behind the window to GUARANTEE position: cushions brake distance error (railBrake) and the rebound folds the landing spread back along the shooting line — a shot wherever the ball stops, even accidentally straight — where a direct route is only in the window at the very end of its travel. The stop shot truly stays put (floor ~1″) and is the easiest of all: it wins whenever staying put is a good solution.
_Avoid_: english, spin shot

**Skill Profile**:
The parameter set describing the assumed player — P(pot) as a function of cut angle and distance, maximum makeable cut, position-speed error — used for both zone boundaries and scoring; v1 ships one fixed intermediate profile. P(pot) combines amplified aim error with object-ball direction noise at contact (throw, cloth roll-off) that is independent of cue-ball distance: the pocket's angular window shrinks with ball-to-pocket distance while that noise stays, so a long pot is punished even with the cue ball parked close behind — closer pockets require much less accuracy. Cuts get GRADUALLY harder past ~30° (cutSweetMax/cutGrowth, 2026-06-12 feedback): below it the pot barely changes with angle; past it the effective aim noise grows beyond the geometric 1/cos amplification (~×1.5 at 48°, ~×1.84 at 60°), on the way to the existing hard gates (quarter-ball beyond ~1 m, 60° max).
_Avoid_: difficulty setting

**Run-out Probability**:
The score of a Pattern — the product over its shots of P(pot) × P(reach the next Position Zone); the solver's "best pattern" is the one maximizing it.
_Avoid_: score, fitness, cost

**Golden Scenario**:
A hand-built fixture Layout with a known-correct Pattern (reconstructed from the knowledgebase articles) that the solver must reproduce in tests; the gate for unlocking configurable ball count.

## Relationships

- A **Pattern** contains one shot per remaining ball of a **Layout**, in forced lowest-ball-first order
- Each shot in a **Pattern** targets exactly one pocket and one **Position Zone** (the last shot has no zone)
- A **Route** connects a shot to the next shot's **Position Zone**
- A **Layout** is only presented to the user if the solver finds at least one complete **Pattern** for it (solver-validated generation; rejected layouts are re-rolled)

## Example dialogue

> **Dev:** "Does the solver pick which ball to shoot first?"
> **Domain expert:** "No — 9-ball forces lowest-ball-first. A **Pattern** only decides pockets, **Routes**, and which **Position Zone** (and which side of it) to land in."

## Flagged ambiguities

- "position window" vs "position zone" — resolved: canonical term is **Position Zone**, matching the PoolDawg pie-shaped-zone concept.
