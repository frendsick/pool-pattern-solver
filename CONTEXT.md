# Pool Pattern Solver

A 9-ball pattern-play trainer: generates random late-rack layouts, solves for the best run-out pattern, and draws it in the style of the PoolDawg pattern-play diagrams (pie-shaped position windows, arrowed cue-ball paths).

## Language

**Pattern**:
A complete run-out plan: the ball-in-hand cue-ball placement, then for each remaining ball the chosen pocket, the cue-ball route to the next shot, and the targeted position window.
_Avoid_: run, sequence, order (order is forced by 9-ball rules and is not a decision)

**Layout**:
A randomly generated set of remaining object-ball positions (no cue ball — the player has ball in hand) that the solver takes as input.
_Avoid_: position (reserved for cue-ball position play), rack

**Ball in Hand**:
The starting condition of every Layout: the opening cue-ball placement can be chosen by the solver or by the player before the first shot, and either way it is the first decision of the Pattern. Placement is exact, so the first shot's intended carom path is far more predictable than from an arbitrary leave — there is no inherited angle to read off the table, the player spots the cue ball for a rehearsed shot (2026-06-12 round 20: "we can be super accurate with the intended cueball carom path because we can spot the ball to the perfect position"). The non-cushion part of the first route's direction noise shrinks accordingly (SkillProfile.handDirEase; rebound noise stays the table's). The placement search exploits this deliberately: besides the fixed angle×distance seed grid (which quantizes the cut 10–15° apart), it SOLVES for the cut angles whose carom path — directly or off one cushion fold — runs along a next-ball shot line (alignedCuts in solver.ts), so "come into the line" plans like the image-#31 top-rail follow fold exist as candidates instead of falling between grid angles. On sortKey near-ties, window entries along the next shot line are preferred over entries that cut across it (alignBoost — the along-the-line counterpart of the keep-it-simple discount; never part of the reported score).
_Avoid_: handball, opening Alternative Leave

**Position Window**:
The region of cue-ball positions from which the next ball can be made to its chosen pocket AND the cue ball can still be moved on toward the following ball's window (onward control). It is ONE concept with two representations: the `zoneValue` SCORING FIELD (the code's `zone*` names) and the DRAWN POLYGON the renderer approximates it with. The polygon must converge to the field; where they disagree (a render-vs-scoring seam — the morphological closing can fill a sub-bar dip, the opening can shave a sliver) it is a BUG to fix, not a second concept. Two invariants govern it (2026-06-15, surfaced by the cue-ball drag feature, which is a zone-tightness diagnostic built on them): FEASIBILITY — every position in the zone affords a makeable shot that reaches the next zone; and UNIFORM EFFECTIVENESS — every position is nearly as effective as the best, so a leave dragged anywhere in the Window barely moves the full-rack run-out. Where dragging swings it materially, the zone is too LOOSE: the relative quality bar (within 80% of the zone's best) or the onward-control gate feeding it must tighten — that is a calibration bug, never a UI state to handle. The zone is bounded by obstacle balls, pocketability/cut limits (max cut 60°, beyond a quarter-ball hit ~48° only within ~1 m), a ~13 cm rail band excluded only where the shot would cue AWAY from the near cushion toward the table center (the bridge hand lands on the rail; shooting along — or into — the near rail is comfortable however close the cushion sits, and such positions stay in the zone) unless the zone exists nowhere else, and a ~25 cm clearance from the object ball itself (a cue ball finishing on top of the next ball is cramped, hard inside ~10 cm) (PoolDawg's "pie-shaped diagram"). The drawn zone is where you'd be HAPPY, not everywhere the shot merely exists: only positions within 80% of the best the zone offers count. Another ball between the zone and the object ball casts a SHADOW — you cannot shoot through it — and the wedge of cue positions it screens from the ghost ball is DEAD: the drawn window is carved around it and never bridges across the blocked directions, and when a ball cuts CLEAN ACROSS the window only the one lobe the route plays for is kept (2026-06-12 image #25: the 9 screening the middle of the 8's window). The pinch can also be RADIAL — a mid-ray dip below the quality bar (e.g. another ball's clearance ring crossing the window): a shallow still-playable below-bar dip is BRIDGED, because the drawn window is the morphological CLOSING of the in-bar core — shallow below-bar gaps bracketed by core along a ray or across the fan are filled, so a stripey fan becomes one uniform window, while weak dips break the drag-clamped polygon — and a rich outer stretch survives a pinch in front of it instead of being clipped at the first dip (2026-06-12 round 17, seed 63: the route search landed in an outer stretch the drawn window had truncated; 2026-06-15 issue #12: drag probing tightened closing so weak dips no longer count as uniform). Only a DEAD gap breaks the window; an angular OPENING then dissolves thin outward mishit slivers, sparing a disk around the route's landing, and the single played lobe is drawn (2026-06-13 round 25, a RENDER-ONLY builder rewrite: zoneValue and scoring are untouched). Onward control discounts an exit by the travel the pot FORCES on the cue ball (pocket pace at the cut angle, exp(-t/~115 cm)) and by the exit type's execution reliability (draw is always the toughest); travel chosen beyond the forced minimum costs nothing — except the hit power it demands (see Route), which prices long exits off near-straight shots out — so the window stays long along the line of the shot, away from the object ball, and natural multi-rail routes count fully. Zones via pockets other than the chosen one are drawn fainter as second-choice expansions, held to the primary pocket's quality bar. The route search compares pockets and shot types on one EFFECTIVE scale: each landing's zone value priced by the route's ease (the type's execution reliability, the hit power the chosen travel demands at that cut, draw rail-room), and a landing counts only within 80% of the best effective value the node can reach over every pocket × type × travel (per-line fallback bars when nothing clears it, so layouts still solve). The closest/easiest pocket still sets the standard, but the hardest shot's access to a zone's richest spot can no longer prune an easy natural follow into a merely decent stretch (2026-06-11 round 8: a long draw chosen over the player's natural-angle follow; 2026-06-12: a tiny window via the planned route when a huge one was reachable). Onward control is computed BACKWARD FROM THE 9 (ADR-0004, 2026-06-12: "in the real world I prefer calculation from the last ball to know what requirements I have for the previous shots"): each ball's value surface is gated by the next ball's surface down to the pot-only 9, each normalized to its own peak ("reach a spot worth ≥60% of the best still available for the REST of the rack"), so the window drawn for the 7 already excludes spots from which the 8 is pottable but only into 8-on-9 dead positions — not one ball of lookahead. The gate is a feasibility prior, not a maximizer (it saturates): it filters dead futures but never ranks near-tied candidates — ranking ball-in-hand placements by it regressed the side-hanger golden by burying the angled placement that flows to the next ball under a rigid straight-in one. The route search runs on the SAME backward-gated zones the renderer draws (shared per-layout surfaces, value.ts; onward control additionally memoized per zone — it varies only with the cut angle, its side, and draw distance, since every exit traces from the fixed ghost ball), and its strict pass keeps landings out of the rail no-go band with a small inset. The drawn window's quality bar is capped to what the chosen route's landing stretch can actually reach (PlannedShot.windowRef): the window shows the stretch that route is playing for, and the planned landing always sits inside it (2026-06-11 screenshot report: a landing in the dead gap between two zone lobes). When the shot is easy and quite short, the headroom goes to AGGRESSION (2026-06-12 round 10): the landing candidates always include the deep end of the route's in-window stretch — usually its closest approach to the next ball — backed off the window's edge by 2σ of landing spread, never to the literal edge; the spread quadrature prices the margin, so the deep landing (maximum draw included, now realistically priced when short) wins exactly when getting 0.5–1 m closer turns a missable next shot into an effectively unmissable one. A CLOSENESS preference (2026-06-13 round 24, `proximity`, seed 149894255: "I would not leave a full-table shot when I can easily get to the middle of the table; penalize the over-half-table shots progressively; the mistake is leaving ourselves too straight on the four — the window should be tighter, leaving an angle from which we get more aggressively to the five") devalues a leave whose cue-to-object distance exceeds half the long rail, falling progressively to half value at the far diagonal and flat (untouched) inside half-table so the comfortable-range stay-in-window and keep-it-simple calibrations are undisturbed: pot probability saturates for easy balls, so without it the value is blind to distance and a dead-straight, full-table leave scores as well as a closer, angled one. Riding on the shared `zoneValue`, it tightens the drawn windows and, propagating through the backward surfaces, makes onward control devalue a straight leave whose only cheap exit stays full-table from the next ball — so the window for the ball before favors an angle that flows toward it.
_Avoid_: shape zone, position zone (use **Position Window** — "zone"/`zoneValue` is only the internal name of its scoring field, not a separate concept)

**Alternative Leave**:
A cue-ball position the player drags to, within a **Position Window**, to ask "from exactly here, what is the best continuation?". The shot played from it is treated as spotted-ball accurate (the ball-in-hand carom ease) even mid-rack, because the drag posits a KNOWN-EXACT placement rather than a noisily-arrived one (ADR-0005, 2026-06-15); only that first shot is spotted, the re-solved continuation after it carries normal arrival noise. Dropping it FORKS the **Pattern**: shots up to the current ball stay as history, and the rest of the rack is re-solved from the Alternative Leave as the new continuation. While dragging, a live single-leg preview re-routes to the current next Window; on release the full remainder re-solves. The player can drag again downstream to fork further, or restore the solver's original line.
_Avoid_: what-if, ghost placement

**Route**:
How the cue ball travels from one shot to the next: a Shot Type plus rails contacted, with travel distance (speed) as the free parameter — bounded below by pocket pace (the object ball must still reach the pocket with margin, so the cue ball cannot travel less than its physics share of that speed) and priced above by hit power: the cue ball keeps only the sin²(cut) + k²·cos²(cut) share of the hit's distance budget (k = the type's roll/draw fraction, 2/7 for follow and draw), so the chosen travel implies an equivalent hit roll-out (hitDistance); the route's value decays past a comfortable stroke (~300″ — a firm one-rail position follow is a routine stroke, not a power shot; taxing one overturned the along-the-window route in 2026-06-12 image #30, and the 13° top-rail fold down the next shot line of image #31 prices at 383″ and is a stroke the player chooses on purpose) and dies at the ceiling (~700″). A near-straight shot keeps almost nothing — sending the cue ball any real distance sideways off one would need a monster stroke that also wrecks the pot, so such routes are not shots. The FINAL ball has a Route too, but no next Position Window to reach: it is chosen for SAFETY — the safest minimal-natural departure (the pocket × Shot Type maximizing P(pot) × P(no scratch) at a soft, position-free stroke), with scratch priced through the same pocketRisk machinery as mid-rack scratch — so the diagram (drawn statically, like every other shot) and the playback both show the cue ball is not heading into a pocket (2026-06-17).

**Shot Type**:
One of five idealized vertical cue-ball actions — stop, follow (natural roll angle), stun (tangent line), touch of low (slight draw, just off the tangent), draw — that fixes the cue ball's departure direction off the object ball. The PATH into that direction is the 30-degree-rule shape (ADR-0003): off the ball the cue ball rides the TANGENT LINE and cloth friction bends it on a small parabola into the carom line — the shape is speed-invariant and scales with the chosen travel, so a harder shot stays on the tangent line farther in absolute inches. Follow visibly bends forward, draw hooks back, stun stays on the tangent; drawn routes, scratch checks and obstacle clearance near the object ball use the real curved path (caromCurve), while candidate landings are walked along the straight landing locus the scaling implies (caromLocus). Each type has an execution reliability (stop 0.99 … draw 0.85 at ~1 m) that multiplies a route's P(position): draw is always the toughest of the available shots, gets worse beyond ~1 m of cue-to-ball distance (it needs a much harder stroke) but EASES below it (drawShortEase: keeping backspin over half a meter is routine, ~0.92 — only the clean-action reliability eases, the landing spread keeps its draw-sized sigmas, so draw stays the hardest type to land precisely), and needs ROOM for the backspin to act — a departure line meeting a cushion within ~10″ compromises the action (drawRailFactor; a touch of low half as much). The carom DIRECTION each type leaves is differently controllable (caromDirSigma, folded into the landing spread): with aim error e the impact line rotates by the amplified A·e, and the departure rotates by a′(cut)·e + (1−a′)·A·e where a′ = d/dc atan(tan c / k). For a rolling follow a′ = 1 exactly at the ~28° natural angle, so the carom direction error collapses to the bare aim error — the 30°-rule plateau, and why a 15–30° cut is the sweet spot for MOVING the cue ball sideways (2026-06-12 feedback) while the kill-angle sweet spot (stopping near in place) is under ~10°, where the stop shot lives. Stun carries the full amplified error, draw more, and a near-straight follow is twitchy (a′ = 3.5) — capped by what the pocket window admits, since bigger errors are misses, not position errors. Routes that skim a pocket mouth carry a scratch-risk penalty — but only where the path ARRIVES within the pocket's acceptance cone: the jaws funnel a ball heading into the mouth, while a pass beyond the acceptance angle (e.g. rebounding off the cushion half a diamond from a side pocket) meets cushion-backed facings and is safe (2026-06-12 round 12: the distance-only penalty shaved a bottom-rail follow that comes back up the next shot's line — the play a player would choose from ball in hand — and let a left-rail follow that merely crosses the window win). Extra travel is only a tie-break, NOT a cost: even a short positional touch carries a ~3–4″ landing floor (speedSigmaFloor — a small window is never guaranteed), so going longer on a natural angle for a significantly bigger window, or to come in along the next shot's line, is often the easier play, and the score's landing-distribution math decides it. Pros drive the cue ball into a cushion behind the window to GUARANTEE position: cushions brake distance error (railBrake) and the rebound folds the landing spread back along the shooting line — a shot wherever the ball stops, even accidentally straight — where a direct route is only in the window at the very end of its travel. The stop shot is the easiest of all and wins whenever staying put is a good solution — but only up close (floor ~1″): killing the cue ball exactly gets harder with distance (the backspin must zero out precisely at contact), so its landing spread grows with cue-to-ball distance (stopDrift, ~3.5″ sigma at 40″) ALONG THE AIM LINE, symmetrically — an under-killed ball creeps forward, toward the pocket the object ball just dropped in (the follow-in scratch is priced by the trace), an over-killed one pulls back (2026-06-12 round 15: a 40″ "stop shot — stays put" was chosen over taking an angle that flows to the next ball's window).
_Avoid_: spin shot, english

**Sidespin**:
Signed left/right cue-ball spin, composed with a Shot Type rather than replacing it. Sidespin is a separate control dimension because a player can use left or right sidespin with follow, stun, or draw. Its full effects include cue-ball squirt, cue-ball swerve, object-ball throw, and changed cushion rebound angles (Dr. Dave, Billiards Digest September 2018). Sidespin amount is normalized so `1.0` means maximum practical side offset near the miscue limit; ordinary pattern play should prefer smaller amounts such as `0.5`, half of maximum sidespin, unless the route clearly needs more. Positive sidespin means right spin; negative sidespin means left spin. Sidespin has its own execution cost and extra cushion-rebound uncertainty so the solver treats it as a useful but more demanding control. Player-facing explanations should say "left spin" or "right spin".
_Avoid_: english, spin shot

**Skill Profile**:
The parameter set describing the assumed player — P(pot) as a function of cut angle and distance, maximum makeable cut, position-speed error — used for both zone boundaries and scoring; v1 ships one fixed intermediate profile. P(pot) combines amplified aim error with object-ball direction noise at contact (throw, cloth roll-off) that is independent of cue-ball distance: the pocket's angular window shrinks with ball-to-pocket distance while that noise stays, so a long pot is punished even with the cue ball parked close behind — closer pockets require much less accuracy. That contact noise is calibrated to make MID-RANGE pots honestly missable (throwSigma 0.02, 2026-06-12 round 16): a 45″ dead-straight 9 reads ~92–95% even from perfect position (was 96–99%) while a ball hanging a foot from a pocket stays a formality, so a pattern that finishes on a near-hanging side-pocket 9 from its big window now beats parking close behind for a mid-range straight one. Cuts get GRADUALLY harder past ~30° (cutSweetMax/cutGrowth, 2026-06-12 feedback): below it the pot barely changes with angle; past it the effective aim noise grows beyond the geometric 1/cos amplification (~×1.5 at 48°, ~×1.84 at 60°), on the way to the existing hard gates (quarter-ball beyond ~1 m, 60° max). A pocket's acceptance cone (side 38°, corner 52°) is a FAR-FIELD property — at distance, a steeper approach meets the jaw facings and stays out — but up close the cone logic stops applying: the visible mouth itself is the target, and a ball near the jaws can be cut in at angles the cone forbids, so the effective acceptance widens toward ~75° as the ball nears the mouth, fading back over ~9″ (effectiveAcceptance; 2026-06-12 image #26: an 8 hanging 5.5″ from the side pocket at 45° off the facing read as unpottable, sending the 8 cross-table to a corner — the solver must prioritize the closest pockets the way a player does; range 6″ → 9″ for image #29 round 17, where a 7 hanging 8.5″ out at ~51° off the facing — "the easy stop shot" — still read as unpottable and was sent 44″ cross-table).
_Avoid_: difficulty setting

**Run-out Probability**:
The score of a Pattern — the product over its shots of P(pot) × P(reach the next Position Window), and for the FINAL ball P(pot) × P(no scratch) since it has no next window to reach. Scratching is an automatic loss, so a 9-ball pot whose only cue routes run into a pocket collapses that leg toward zero — it is not a valid run-out (2026-06-17). The solver's "best pattern" is the one maximizing this product.
_Avoid_: score, fitness, cost

**Golden Scenario**:
A hand-built fixture Layout with a known-correct Pattern (reconstructed from the knowledgebase articles) that the solver must reproduce in tests; the gate for unlocking configurable ball count.

## Relationships

- A **Pattern** contains one shot per remaining ball of a **Layout**, in forced lowest-ball-first order
- Each shot in a **Pattern** targets exactly one pocket and one **Position Window** (the last shot has no window)
- A **Route** connects a shot to the next shot's **Position Window** — except the final shot's Route, which has no Window and is chosen to avoid a scratch
- A **Position Window** is one concept with two representations — the `zoneValue` scoring field and the drawn polygon; the cue-ball drag clamps to the drawn polygon but scores on the field
- An **Alternative Leave** forks a **Pattern** from the current ball: earlier shots are retained as history, the remainder re-solved from the dragged placement
- A **Layout** is only presented to the user if the solver finds at least one complete **Pattern** for it (solver-validated generation; rejected layouts are re-rolled)

## Example dialogue

> **Dev:** "Does the solver pick which ball to shoot first?"
> **Domain expert:** "No — 9-ball forces lowest-ball-first. A **Pattern** only decides pockets, **Routes**, and which **Position Window** (and which side of it) to land in."

## Flagged ambiguities

- "position window" vs "position zone" — canonical term is **Position Window** (2026-06-15, flipped from the earlier "Position Zone" choice to match how the player speaks). It is ONE concept; "zone"/`zoneValue` survives only as the internal name of the scoring FIELD the window is computed from (and the code's `zone*` identifiers). The drawn polygon (round 25's render-only artifact) must converge to that field — the cue-ball drag clamps to the polygon but scores on the field, and any gap between them is a render-vs-scoring bug to fix, not a second concept.
